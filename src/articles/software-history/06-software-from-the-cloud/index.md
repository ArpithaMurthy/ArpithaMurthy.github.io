# Software Becomes a Service: The Cloud Era

Before cloud computing, organizations bought servers, installed software, and planned for the busiest day they could imagine. That gave them direct control, but also meant buying, housing, maintaining, and scaling the machines themselves.

Cloud computing changed the arrangement. Instead of owning every layer, a team could rent computing, storage, databases, and ready-made software over a network.

## The computer behind the app moves away

The Web had already taught people to reach services through a browser. Cloud infrastructure made it easier for the companies behind those services to provision computing resources when needed. The National Institute of Standards and Technology describes cloud computing through traits such as on-demand access, shared resources, and rapid provisioning.

This made new business models practical. Software as a Service (SaaS) let customers use an application maintained by its provider, often through a subscription. Platform as a Service and Infrastructure as a Service offered developers and organizations different levels of control over the software and machines underneath.

## Build without owning the whole stack

A small team could launch a website without first purchasing a room of servers. A company could handle sudden growth by adding capacity. Developers increasingly assembled products from managed databases, identity providers, payment services, messaging platforms, and APIs.

This is a new kind of software story: the application is not one program on one computer. It is a service made from networks of services, teams, data, and contracts. A button on a phone may trigger code in a cloud region, call a payment provider, update a database, and send a notification.

## Complexity did not vanish

Cloud services reduce some kinds of work, but they do not remove responsibility. Teams still need to secure accounts, protect data, manage outages, control costs, and understand what happens when a provider fails. Renting a machine changes who operates it; it does not make the system's consequences disappear.

## What changed for people?

Software became something that could be continuously updated and reached from almost anywhere. Teams could build globally available products with less upfront infrastructure. The next change would reach beyond accessing software as a service: what if the software could create a first draft, image, answer, or plan on request?
